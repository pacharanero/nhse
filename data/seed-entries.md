## 2019-07 - Palantir UK hosts NHS England chair before NHSX launch

- category: `contract`
- date_precision: `month`
- verified: `secondary`
- source: BBC News, "Palantir: NHS faces legal action over data firm contract", 24 Feb 2021 - <https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/technology-56183785>
- originally reported by: Bureau of Investigative Journalism with Sky News

Palantir's UK chief hosted Lord David Prior, then chair of NHS England, the night before the launch of NHSX. Prior emailed thanks for the "interesting dinner and also for the watermelon cocktails", which Sky reported cost about £60. Exchanges between the NHS and Palantir followed over subsequent months, preceding the first contract at the outset of the pandemic.

`contested`: Peter Smith, former president of the Chartered Institute of Procurement and Supply, told the BBC "It doesn't look great, but all the big suppliers to government do it", and that it would be wrong to say no senior official should ever meet a prospective supplier. Sequence only - no causal link is established.

## 2023-08 - NHS England chief data officer lobbies the National Data Guardian

- category: `official`
- date_precision: `month`
- verified: `secondary`
- source: FT, "NHS executive lobbied data watchdog to rewrite criticism before Palantir deal", Borrett and Hughes, 18 Sep 2026 - <https://www.ft.com/content/63bfdeb2-efb1-4e3a-8c89-a884eb5bd618>
- paywalled: `true`
- primary source outstanding: the FOI disclosure of the August 2023 emails

Byrne shared an advance copy of her FDP statement with NHS England. Ming Tang, then chief data officer, replied: "I am really disappointed that you wish to publish this document as the content will create significant problems for me and the programme." After a phone call, Tang sent feedback including requests to soften "very inflammatory" language about the absence of a communications strategy and to "provide balance" by acknowledging that concerns about privacy, procurement and commercial matters were "not well founded". Byrne delayed publication "to allow for a substantial rewrite of the framing of the blog given the concerns you raised, and having seen that there is now a communications plan".

`contested`: The Office of the National Data Guardian says pre-publication sharing is "normal practice", that it retained editorial control, that Byrne "upheld her independence and impartiality", and that the published version was "as robust and comprehensive as the draft". NHS England says the NDG "operates entirely independently of NHS England and there was no expectation that changes would be made to the blog, other than to correct any factual inaccuracies". Ming Tang did not respond to the FT.

## 2023-08-24 - National Data Guardian publishes the FDP blog

- category: `official`
- date_precision: `day`
- verified: `document`
- source: <https://www.gov.uk/government/news/the-nhs-federated-data-platform-the-importance-of-building-bridges-with-the-public> (OGL v3)

Byrne publishes "The NHS Federated Data Platform: the importance of building bridges with the public", setting out three areas NHS England must address publicly. Among the questions she says need answering to the public's satisfaction:

> "where does public choice come into play, and how will the national data opt-out work?"

She also warns on lock-in, asking whether the NHS "can terminate the partnership as and when necessary, without compromising patient care or incurring significant costs", and on opt-outs: "If opt-out rates now rise considerably further, it would be to the serious detriment of health research and planning nationally."

`related`: the 2023-08 lobbying entry. Draft-vs-published comparison is worked through in the EDH episode folder; part of the rewrite is a defensible factual correction (the draft complained of a missing communications plan which by then existed), part is tone-only.

## 2023-12-07 - Ming Tang tells the board opt-out is being redefined

- category: `board`
- date_precision: `day`
- verified: `caption` - **must be listened to before use**
- video: `W6ZGpIS4jlA`, t=6437
- source: <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W6ZGpIS4jlA&t=6437>

At the first board meeting after the award, answering a board member on patients who opt out of data sharing, Ming Tang says NHS England is "checking in what the views of opt out with the joint data policy unit" and is "also re-looking at what does opt out mean and how do we really make sure that's aligned and understood by patients."

She adds that opt-out levels "have not really materialised as much as we feared", attributing a peak to "Foxglove and other privacy campaigners", and that messaging was tested so people understand "that for direct care, it's not in their interest to opt out, obviously they can, but it's unlikely" (t=6508).

## 2023-12-07 - Ming Tang on primary care data at national level

- category: `scope`
- date_precision: `day`
- verified: `caption` - **must be listened to before use**
- video: `W6ZGpIS4jlA`, t=6175

"None of the data sharing arrangements are changing... There's been lots of questions around whether FDP will include primary care data... If a local system in an ICS has data sharing agreements with their primary care... then absolutely primary care data can go in. At a national level we don't have a national feed of primary care data."

Note the qualification - "at a national level", "in the national use cases". This is the same distinction the April 2026 FAQ later relies on, so the statements are not flatly contradictory. The scope question is whether the public heard the qualification.
