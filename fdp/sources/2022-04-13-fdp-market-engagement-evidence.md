## Scope passages

Slide 13 separates four uses of data:

- Direct care: clinical and care staff and citizens; "Real time (read/write access)"; "Must be identifiable"; linked event-based patient records, appointments and pathways, plans, communications, and safeguarding data.
- Population health and proactive care: daily or real-time data; identifiable data for authorised staff where needed to enable direct care; linked clinical, demographic, wider social-determinant, omic and outcome data.
- Planning, oversight and service improvement: NHS, care, local-authority and public-sector users; data "Must not be identifiable"; capacity, demand, activity, workforce, financial, safety and asset data.
- Research and innovation: academic, charity and industry researchers; normally anonymised data, with "Re-identifiable data, imperfect but timely" for trial recruitment and follow-up.

The slide concludes that these uses are "rooted in linked person-level data". Slide 14 maps vaccination and immunisation, elective recovery, and care coordination to direct care. It maps research support through a link to Trusted Research Environments.

The Q&A says the FDP "will not be a Trusted Research Environment (TRE)" but would provide source data to planned TREs. It also says the common data model would be aligned to specific use cases because NHS England was "not intending to use the FDP to collect wholesale data not relevant to the specific use cases".

The RFI says the FDP would connect local provider instances and "ensure that control of data does not change and remains with the organisations responsible for, and close to, the delivery of care".

## Legal and governance omissions

The documents do not identify a UK GDPR Article 6 basis, Article 9 condition, common-law confidentiality route, National Data Opt-Out position, Type 1 opt-out position, or formal controller/processor allocation for patient-data processing. Their references to procurement law, freedom of information law and NHS England's general statutory role are not presented as patient-data lawful bases. This is evidence of what the market-engagement material did and did not state, not a finding that no lawful basis existed.

## Other quantified claims

The deck says the FDP had "the potential to deliver £3.6bn benefits over 10 years" without supplying a supporting methodology in this set. It estimates Procurement 1 Lot A at £360 million over five years and Lot B, privacy-enhancing technology, at £10 million. These component figures should not be treated as directly comparable with the £240 million estimate in the 1 April Prior Information Notice without reconciling their scope.
